# Phase 1 — Business Context

## 1.1 The organization

**Atlas Rehabilitation Network (ARN)** is a (fictional) private outpatient
physiotherapy and rehabilitation provider operating **8 clinics** across five
Spanish cities (Madrid ×2, Barcelona ×2, Valencia, Sevilla, Bilbao, Málaga).
The network employs **~110 physiotherapists** and delivers roughly **45,000
patient visits per year**, mostly funded through a mix of public agreements
(SNS), private insurers and self-pay.

ARN runs an electronic health record (EHR) that exposes a **FHIR R4 API**, but
clinical and operational reporting is fragmented: each clinic manager works from
spreadsheets exported manually, there is no shared definition of "drop-out" or
"recovery", and the executive team has no consolidated view of outcomes. ARN
created a **Health Informatics Analyst** function to close that gap. This
project is the work product of that role.

> All data in this repository is **synthetic** (generated by
> `data/generate_synthetic_data.py`). No real patient data is used. The figures
> quoted throughout the documentation are computed from that synthetic dataset
> and are reproducible.

## 1.2 Problems the platform addresses

ARN faces four recurring problems that are common to real physiotherapy
networks and directly affect both patients and the bottom line:

1. **Lost capacity to no-shows and late cancellations.** Missed appointments
   waste clinician time that cannot be recovered and delay other patients'
   access. ARN had no reliable, comparable measure of the problem across
   clinics.

2. **Treatment drop-out / non-adherence.** A meaningful share of patients stop
   attending before completing their plan. Incomplete rehabilitation is
   associated with worse recovery and avoidable re-referral, yet ARN could not
   identify *who* was drifting away *while there was still time to intervene*.

3. **Unmeasured and variable clinical outcomes.** Physiotherapists collected
   patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) inconsistently, on paper, using
   different scales per body region. There was no way to answer "are our
   patients actually getting better, and where do we do this best?"

4. **No single source of truth for decision-making.** Operational, clinical and
   financial questions required manual data pulls, so reporting was slow,
   inconsistent and impossible to trust at board level.

## 1.3 Strategic objectives

| # | Objective | How the platform supports it |
|---|-----------|------------------------------|
| O1 | **Increase usable clinical capacity** by reducing no-shows and late cancellations | Quantify and monitor no-show/cancellation drivers; enable targeted reminders and overbooking policy |
| O2 | **Improve treatment completion** and reduce avoidable drop-out | A weekly drop-out risk register that flags active patients with attendance gaps |
| O3 | **Make recovery measurable and comparable** across clinics, conditions and therapists | A harmonised PROM model (baseline→discharge) with MCID-based outcome KPIs |
| O4 | **Standardise reporting** on a single, governed analytics layer | FHIR → SQL → Power BI pipeline feeding executive, clinical and operational dashboards |
| O5 | **Lay the groundwork for predictive analytics** (no-show and drop-out prediction) under EU governance | Clean, modelled data and documented, auditable definitions |

| **Network Operations Director** | Capacity, throughput, cost | No-show/cancellation rates, utilisation, waiting times |

## 1.5 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

These KPIs are defined once here and implemented consistently in SQL (`/sql`)
and DAX (`/powerbi`). Targets are illustrative benchmarks for a network of this
type.

| KPI | Definition / formula | Owner | Illustrative target |
|-----|----------------------|-------|---------------------|
| **No-show rate** | `noshow / (fulfilled + noshow + cancelled)` | Operations | < 10% |
| **Cancellation rate** | `cancelled / (fulfilled + noshow + cancelled)` | Operations | < 6% |
| **Lost capacity** | `(noshow + cancelled) / resolved appointments` | Operations | < 15% |
| **Treatment adherence** | `attended_sessions / planned_sessions` per episode | Clinical | ≥ 80% |
| **Completion rate** | `completed / (completed + revoked)` episodes | Clinical | ≥ 80% |
| **Drop-out rate** | `revoked / (completed + revoked)` episodes | Clinical | < 20% |
| **Mean time to discharge** | `discharge_date − episode_start` (completed episodes) | Clinical | condition-specific |
| **Functional MCID achievement** | % of episodes whose change in functional PROM ≥ instrument MCID | Clinical | ≥ 80% |
| **Pain MCID achievement** | % of episodes with NPRS reduction ≥ 2 points | Clinical | ≥ 80% |
| **Patients at risk of drop-out** | active episodes with ≥14 days since last visit and sessions remaining | Clinic managers | minimise |

### A note on outcome measurement (PROMs and MCID)

Physiotherapy outcomes are measured with validated **patient-reported outcome
measures**. This project uses the instruments routinely used in MSK practice and
maps each to a LOINC code:

| Instrument | Used for | Scale | MCID used here |
|------------|----------|-------|----------------|
| **NPRS** (Numeric Pain Rating Scale) | Pain, all conditions | 0–10 (higher = worse) | 2 points |
| **ODI** (Oswestry Disability Index) | Low back pain, sciatica | 0–100% (higher = worse) | 10 points |
| **NDI** (Neck Disability Index) | Neck pain | 0–100% (higher = worse) | 7 points |
| **QuickDASH** | Shoulder, elbow, wrist | 0–100 (higher = worse) | 15 points |
| **LEFS** (Lower Extremity Functional Scale) | Knee, hip, ankle, foot | 0–80 (higher = **better**) | 9 points |

**MCID (Minimal Clinically Important Difference)** is the smallest change a
patient perceives as meaningful. Reporting "% of patients reaching MCID" is far
more clinically honest than reporting an average point change, because it counts
*patients who actually improved enough to notice*. To allow cross-condition
comparison, all functional instruments are also harmonised to a common
**0–100 disability scale (higher = worse)** in the analytics layer (see
`fact_outcome` in `/sql/03_star_schema.sql`).

## 1.6 Scope and success criteria

**In scope:** outpatient MSK physiotherapy episodes; scheduling, attendance,
diagnoses, PROMs and care plans; descriptive and diagnostic analytics; three
Power BI dashboards.

**Out of scope (this iteration):** billing/claims, inpatient rehabilitation,
real-time API streaming, and the production deployment of any predictive model
(scoped conceptually in Phase 7).

**Success criteria:** (1) a single governed data model from FHIR to BI;
(2) every KPI defined once and reproducible in SQL; (3) dashboards that answer
the stakeholders' questions above; (4) at least one actionable, quantified
insight per problem area (delivered in Phase 6).
